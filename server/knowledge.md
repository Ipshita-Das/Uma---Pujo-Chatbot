## The days of Pujo

- **Mahalaya**: marks the end of Pitri Paksha and the start of Devi Paksha. Many Bengalis wake before dawn to hear "Mahishasura Mardini" on All India Radio (Akashvani), with the famous recitation by Birendra Krishna Bhadra. Tarpan is offered to ancestors at the ghats.
- **Shashthi**: Bodhon (awakening of the goddess), Amantran and Adhibas. The faces of the idols are unveiled at many pandals.
- **Saptami**: Nabapatrika (Kola Bou) is bathed at dawn, often in the Ganga, dressed in a red-bordered sari and placed beside Ganesh. Main puja begins.
- **Ashtami**: the biggest day. Morning Pushpanjali (flower offering), Kumari Puja at some places, and Sandhi Puja at the junction of Ashtami and Nabami, offered with 108 lamps and 108 lotuses.
- **Nabami**: Maha Nabami puja and bhog; the last full night of pandal hopping, often called "Nabami nishi".
- **Dashami (Bijoya Dashami)**: Darpan Bisarjan, Debi Boron and Sindoor Khela by married women, then immersion (bisarjan). Afterwards people do Bijoya greetings: pranam to elders, kolakuli (embraces), and sweets.
- **Kojagari Lakshmi Puja** follows on the full moon after Dashami.

## Durga and her family

- Durga slays the buffalo demon Mahishasura; she rides a lion and holds weapons given by the gods in her ten hands (trishul, chakra, sword, bow and arrow, conch, and more).
- She comes to her parents' home with her children: Lakshmi (owl), Saraswati (swan), Ganesh (mouse) and Kartik (peacock).
- The Bengali "ekchala" arrangement places the whole family under one arched backdrop, the chalchitra.

## Heritage

- Kolkata's Durga Puja was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2021.
- Kumartuli in North Kolkata is the traditional potters' quarter where most idols are sculpted from clay and straw.
- Bonedi bari (old family) pujos keep older rituals and are held in the thakur dalan of family mansions. Well-known examples: Shobhabazar Rajbari, Rani Rashmoni's house in Janbazar, Laha Bari, Chhatu Babu-Latu Babu's house, and Mallick Bari.
- Sarbojanin (community) pujos run by neighbourhood clubs grew through the 20th century; Bagbazar Sarbojanin is among the oldest.

## Food

- **Bhog**: khichuri, labra, beguni, tomato chutney, payesh. Many families keep niramish (vegetarian) meals on some days, especially Ashtami.
- **Street food**: phuchka, kathi rolls, mughlai paratha, fish fry, chop, ghugni.
- **Mishti**: rosogolla, sandesh, mishti doi, nolen gurer sweets later in the season.

## Pujo fashion by day

- **Shashthi/Saptami**: comfortable festive: cotton tant, handloom kurtas, light panjabi.
- **Ashtami anjali**: traditional: lal-paar sada (white with red border), garad or korial silk; men in panjabi with dhoti or pajama.
- **Nabami evening**: dressiest night: Banarasi, Baluchari, Dhakai jamdani, silk panjabi, Nehru jacket.
- **Dashami**: lal-paar sada or red and white for Sindoor Khela.
- **Accessories**: shakha-pola, jhumka, tip (bindi), alta, gajra (jasmine), gold or oxidised jewellery; kolhapuri or juttis, but comfortable footwear for pandal hopping.

## Practical tips

- Crowds peak in the evenings from Saptami to Nabami; go early morning or very late night.
- Kolkata Metro usually extends its hours during Pujo; check the announced timings.
- Wear comfortable shoes, carry water, a power bank, some cash for small stalls, and keep phones and wallets secure.
- At anjali, remove footwear where asked; traditionally people fast until after anjali. Ask before photographing people.
